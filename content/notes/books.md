## A list of good books

#### Carl Sagan | Pale Blue Dot
An excellent book that overviews much of Carl Sagan's philosophy, and one that shapes my personal philosophy around what humans should strive to achieve. Among the most important points is that the idea of humans holding a privileged place in the Universe is wrong and potentially dangerous, given our vulnerability to extinction events that we may bring upon ourselves or that the Universe may bring upon us. Instead we must always pay attention to our inherent fragility as a thin film of life on a blue speck of dust in the cosmos.
#### Ursula K. Le Guin | The Lathe of Heaven
Le Guin is my favorite sci-fi author, and writes some of the best prose I have read in science fiction. She explores the relation between dreams and reality especially well, and in the Lathe of Heaven explores themes of godly power and who should hold it, if anyone.
#### Cixin Liu | Three Body Problem (Trilogy)
Some of the most imaginative speculative science fiction written recently. Its prose is pretty difficult to work through at times, especially as the books are translated from Chinese, and the second and third books do get longer and read slower, but they grow so much in the scale of their ideas. By the end of each book Liu leaves the reader with a sense of awe about the cosmological theory he imparts.
#### Diane Vaughan | The Challenger Launch Decision
An extremely important dissection of the managerial causes of the Challenger disaster, which should be required reading for all engineers. Vaughan picks apart the simple widespread narrative of NASA managers pressuring Thiokol engineers to approve a launch outside of safe conditions and instead describes a much more complex story of procedural drift and normalization of performance deviance, amplified by an under-resourced and schedule-pressured NASA. A bit of a tough read, but extremely important to be able to pick out the population scale effects of engineering culture that are hard to see as an individual embedded within it.
#### Hua Hsu | Stay True
One of my favorite memoirs, which so perfectly captures the atmosphere of college and young adult life, before a tragic depiction of loss.
#### Anthony Doerr | All the Light We Cannot See
A wonderful book that highlights the power and the fleeting nature of human connection even in times of trouble.
#### Ted Chiang | Exhalation (Short Story Collection)
A collection of crisp short stories in science fiction which explore ideas from the purpose of creation to the nature of memory and choice. He puts uniquely human perspectives to far-out concepts like time travel, entropy, and many-worlds.

My favorites were:
- "Exhalation"
- "Omphalos"
- "Anxiety is the Dizziness of Freedom"
